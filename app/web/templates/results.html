The blind spot: scam payments

Every learned model caught 0% of authorised push payment scams. In this data they look exactly like genuine large transfers to a new payee: the real customer, on their own phone, at home, at a normal hour. The rules caught two thirds only because they flag every payment over £5,000, which is also why they catch more fraud by value ({{ pct(B.fraud_value_caught, 0) }} against the model's {{ pct(L.fraud_value_caught, 0) }}): scams are rare but large.

Adding one guardrail rule (large payment to a new payee) to the model lifts fraud value caught to {{ pct(r['logreg+guardrail'].fraud_value_caught, 0) }}, but precision falls from {{ pct(L.precision, 0) }} to {{ pct(r['logreg+guardrail'].precision, 0) }}. That is worth it only if those alerts trigger something cheap, like a warning screen or a confirmation call. It's measured here and not deployed, because it's a product decision. Real banks add other signals here: Confirmation of Payee, payee account age and intelligence on mule accounts.

Where false alarms come from

Most of the rules' false alarms were customers being themselves. Comparing each payment with that customer's own history removes almost all of them.
